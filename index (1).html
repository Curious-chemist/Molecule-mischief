How does it work?

Three ideas, from tiny to big.

1

Every particle is random

Each molecule moves in a random direction at a random speed. You cannot predict one particle. Its speed is just a number drawn from a bell-shaped (Gaussian) distribution in each direction.

2

The crowd is predictable

Add up thousands of particles and a smooth curve appears: the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. The area under it is 1, because every particle must have some speed (total probability).

f(v) = (v/kT)·e^(−v²/2kT)
3

Crowd + walls = pressure

Pressure is just particles bumping the wall. More heat means faster hits. Less room means more hits. That is the Ideal Gas Law, and the lab checks it live.

P·A = N·kT
∫

Where is calculus?

The average speed is found by integrating v·f(v) from 0 to infinity. The peak is found by setting the derivative f′(v) to zero. That is how we get v_peak = √kT.
